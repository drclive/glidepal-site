The moments that worry people, explained while they happen.

Most of what feels alarming on a flight is ordinary work by the airplane and the crew. Glidepal names it the moment it happens.

After takeoff

"Why did the engines suddenly get quieter?"

The pilots reduce power once the airplane is safely climbing. It's planned and expected, and the climb continues.

Just after liftoff

"What was that thump under the floor?"

The landing gear folding up into the airplane. You'll hear it again, in reverse, before landing.

During the climb

"What does that chime mean?"

A signal to the crew, often as the airplane passes 10,000 feet. Nothing is wrong; it's part of the routine.

In cruise

"Is this turbulence dangerous?"

Airplanes are built for far more than you'll feel. Glidepal tells you when the bumps are light, and when the crew changes altitude to find smoother air.

Starting down

"Why does it feel like we're slowing and dipping?"

The descent has begun. The pilots may raise panels on the wings to slow down, which can add a gentle rumble.

After touchdown

"Why is it so loud right after we land?"

The engines help slow the airplane on the runway. The roar fades within seconds as you slow to taxi speed.

How it works.

1

Before you board

Open Glidepal at the gate, choose your narrator, and put your phone in airplane mode like everyone else. Rest it on the tray table or your seat for the best readings.

2

During the flight

Glidepal uses your phone's own sensors to follow the flight: taxi, takeoff, climb, cruise, descent and landing. It explains each step in a calm voice, with no internet needed.

3

Between flights

Short lessons on how airplanes fly, what turbulence really is, and why pilots do what they do. No news about incidents, ever.
